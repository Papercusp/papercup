Resolution Inbox Redesign

The inbox isn't ugly so much as squeezed out of existence. Four stacked chrome blocks claim their full height before the item list is measured, and the list is the only element allowed to flex — so it absorbs the entire deficit. At a 1280×720 viewport it resolves to zero pixels tall and renders none of its rows, while the detail pane beside it holds 696×655 of empty space.

The mechanism

.op-inbox__master is a flex column. Its masthead, bulk-run report, filter card and window strip are all flex: 0 0 auto; only .op-inbox__list is flex: 1 1 auto with min-height: 0. That is a correct-looking rule set that quietly hands one child every pixel of shortfall — so the shortfall lands entirely on the one thing the screen exists to show.

It compounds: with the container at zero height the virtualised list renders no rows at all, so there is nothing to click, so the detail pane stays on its placeholder. Both halves of the screen go blank together.

B, with an item open

Selecting a row replaces the briefing. Today this state has its own problems, separate from the collapse: the item's title is never shown in the pane at all — only in the row you clicked — the six metadata chips are strewn across 1100px at a contrast that makes three of them near-invisible, the body runs about 200 characters per line, and the whole thing occupies the top fifth of the pane with ~800px of nothing under it.

So the detail splits: a main column held to a 60-character measure, and a metadata rail that gathers the chips into labelled rows you can actually read. Actions sit directly under the body — for a two-sentence item like this one, pinning them to the bottom of a 655px pane just recreates the void in a tidier costume.

Direction C

Treat it as a queue, not a mailbox

The split-pane borrows from email, but this content isn't mail — it's a work queue of near-identical rows that you sort, scan and clear. So drop the split until it's needed: full width, 40px rows, real columns for kind, harness, state and age, and a severity stripe. Sorting by age or state becomes meaningful because the column headers exist. Selecting an item slides the detail in from the right over the table.

Recommendation

Direction B — selected. Here's the build order.

B fixes both halves of the screen at once: the list gets its 533px back because everything explanatory moves right, and the pane that was showing one line of placeholder text becomes the briefing when nothing is selected and a properly-set item when something is. A and C stay on this page as the discarded alternatives — A is the same list fix without the pane work, C is where this goes if bulk selection ever lands.

  1. Land the floor guard first, on its own. A min-height on .op-inbox__list plus a chrome cap on .op-inbox__master stops the collapse today and guards the class from returning — independent of B, reviewable in a minute.
  2. Then the shell: strip the left column to search, filters and queue; add the briefing view to .op-inbox__aside alongside its existing empty and detail states.
  3. Then the detail: show the item title in the pane (it currently never appears there), hold the body to a ~60-character measure, and move the six chips into the metadata rail at full contrast.
  4. Pick one total. 100, 143, 158 and 1540 are four different populations. Show the one the screen is about and put the rest in the briefing.
  5. Drop the blue. The hero, row icons and active filter use #3f7fd6 against an app whose accent is #5fae9f. Nothing else in the portal does this.
