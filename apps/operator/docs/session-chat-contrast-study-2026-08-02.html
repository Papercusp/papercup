Three ways to make the sections read as sections

The ask was two things at once — make it clearer that there are different sections, and raise the contrast between a heading and the text under it. Those are separate levers, so this sheet measures each, then shows three treatments that pull them in different proportions. Structure and general design are unchanged throughout: same rail, same order, same content.

What's actually wrong

Two defects, both measurable

Ratios are computed against the popup ground --bg-popover #0d1829, using the shared token values the sections are built from.

Defect 1 · invisible boundaries

The section divider is a 1.39:1 line

Sections are separated by a single hairline at --border — sky-300 at 15% alpha, which composites to #1e3449 on the panel. At 1.39:1 against its own background it is below every legibility floor there is; the boundary is functionally not drawn. Nothing else in the layout marks a section edge, so the rail reads as one continuous column of small text.

current rule 1.39:1
--border-strong 2.22:1
non-text floor 3.00:1
Defect 2 · inverted hierarchy

The heading is 2.6× dimmer than the text it labels

Headings are --fg-mute at 6.15:1; the body beneath them is --fg at 16.23:1. So the label is the quietest thing in its own section — and it is also the smallest, at 9.5px against the body's 10.5px. Size, weight and colour all point the same wrong way, and only uppercasing pushes back. A heading has to win at least one of those axes to read as a heading.

heading now 6.15:1
body now 16.23:1
step 0.38×

The fix for defect 2 is not "brighten the heading". Both values are already high-contrast against the ground — the problem is that they are close to each other and ordered backwards. Every treatment below therefore moves them apart: heading up to --fg, body down to --fg-dim. That inverts the step from 0.38× to 1.40× without introducing a single new colour.

The trade

What each one costs

All three fix defect 2 identically — they differ only in how hard they push on defect 1, and what that costs in vertical space and visual noise.

Treatment Section signal Vertical cost Risk
A · Type ladder A stronger line, still only a line — 2.22:1, under the 3:1 floor. None. Byte-for-byte the same height. May not go far enough. If the complaint was "I can't see the sections", a brighter hairline is the smallest possible answer to it.
B · Banded headers A filled bar spanning the rail. Separation comes from an area, not a line, so it survives at any zoom. ~2px per section. Rail starts to look striped if a section holds only one row. Mitigated by the bands being subtle (8%) rather than boxed.
C · Inset cards Strongest — each section is a discrete object. ~8px per section, plus 9px of gutter on both edges. Eats horizontal room in a 272px rail, and reads as a different design language from the rest of the popup. This is the one that stops being "same general design".

Recommendation: B. Defect 1 is the one you actually raised first, and it is a boundary problem — a boundary drawn as an area is legible in a way a 1px line at any alpha is not, which is why A tops out under the 3:1 floor while B clears it by construction. B also keeps the rail's structure and rhythm exactly as-is: no new gutters, no rounded boxes, nothing that changes the language of the surrounding popup the way C does.

If B's bands feel too heavy once they're live, the fill drops from 8% to 5% and it degrades gracefully toward A — the two are the same treatment at different strengths, so switching between them later is a one-line change, not a redesign.

Ratios computed from app/_semantic.css token values against --bg-popover, sRGB relative luminance per WCAG 2.1. The Orders rail additionally lightens its own local copies of these tokens (--pc-orders-dim, --pc-orders-text), which narrows the heading/body step but does not reorder it — the heading stays the quieter of the two.
