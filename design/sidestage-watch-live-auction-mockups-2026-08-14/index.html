Watch · live auction · design directions

Keep the room bidding.

Three materially different ways to improve SideStage’s live-auction moment without losing the Watch page’s cream, red, and yellow identity—or the state semantics already proven in the React implementation.

Source-backed, not a reset

Each direction preserves the real live → settling → sold contract, ring timer, accepted-bid feed, leader messaging, sync status, bid form, and server-confirmed winner. Toggle the states inside every mockup.

Direction 02

Watch Dock

Keep bidding attached to the thing people came to watch. A compact dock hugs the livestream, carries the active price and next action, and expands into a touch-friendly stack on narrow screens.

Best forFast rooms where scrolling away from the stream costs bids.
TradeoffMust coordinate with the existing mobile held-item action.

Direction 03

Transparent Room

Treat trust as a feature. The bidding stage says what the room knows, what the server still has to confirm, and why a retry cannot duplicate a bid—while keeping the accepted-bid ledger visible.

Best forHigher-value lots and buyers who need confidence before acting.
TradeoffThe ledger is denser and should collapse progressively on mobile.

Decision support

Same auction truth, different emphasis.

All three preserve the current state machine and SideStage token system. The decision is where the auction should sit in the Watch hierarchy—not whether to replace its backend contract.
